# Author library

This is the reading list the cards will draw from. Each author can supply many cards across many works. The column on the right is the kind of job their lines tend to be good at, so we can keep the toolkit balanced.

★ = already has a card in `cards.json`.

## Classic

| Author | Works to pull from | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Franz Kafka ★ | The Metamorphosis, The Trial, letters and diaries | The impossible stated flatly, dread in office language |
| Anton Chekhov ★ | The Lady with the Dog, Gooseberries, Ward No. 6, letters | Endings that don't resolve, small gestures that carry a life |
| Leo Tolstoy ★ | Anna Karenina, The Death of Ivan Ilyich | Opening claims, inner life in a single movement |
| Fyodor Dostoevsky ★ | Notes from Underground, Crime and Punishment | Unreliable, spiteful, very human narrators |
| Marcel Proust ★ | In Search of Lost Time (Swann's Way, Within a Budding Grove, The Guermantes Way, Time Regained) | Memory, jealousy, long sentences that still land |
| Virginia Woolf ★ | Mrs Dalloway, To the Lighthouse, A Room of One's Own | Time inside a mind, essays that argue gracefully |
| James Joyce ★ | Dubliners (The Dead), Ulysses | Epiphanies, last paragraphs |
| Jane Austen ★ | Pride and Prejudice, Emma, Persuasion | Irony, opening lines, dialogue that cuts |
| Charles Dickens ★ | Bleak House, Great Expectations, A Tale of Two Cities | Fog, crowds, comic character sketches |
| George Orwell ★ | 1984, Animal Farm, Politics and the English Language, Shooting an Elephant | One wrong detail, plain style, rules for writing |
| Ernest Hemingway ★ | A Farewell to Arms, Hills Like White Elephants | Leaving things out, dialogue that hides the subject |
| F. Scott Fitzgerald ★ | The Great Gatsby | Longing, closing lines, describing a party |
| James Baldwin ★ | Notes of a Native Son, Giovanni's Room, The Fire Next Time | Anger with control, the essay as argument |
| Flannery O'Connor ★ | A Good Man Is Hard to Find, Mystery and Manners | Violence and comedy in the same paragraph |
| Albert Camus ★ | The Stranger, The Myth of Sisyphus | Detached narrators, the first line as a shock |
| Jorge Luis Borges ★ | Ficciones, Labyrinths | Ideas as stories, the fake footnote |
| Italo Calvino ★ | Invisible Cities, If on a Winter's Night a Traveller | Talking straight to the reader, describing the invisible |
| Gabriel García Márquez ★ | One Hundred Years of Solitude, Chronicle of a Death Foretold | Openings that promise, magic told with a straight face |
| Vladimir Nabokov ★ | Lolita, Pnin, Speak, Memory | Sound, seductive narrators, precise detail |
| Sylvia Plath ★ | The Bell Jar, journals | Private and public dread in the same breath |
| R. K. Narayan | Swami and Friends, The Guide, Malgudi Days | Gentle comedy, a whole town in a paragraph |
| Saadat Hasan Manto | Toba Tek Singh, Thanda Gosht (in translation) | The short story as a gut punch, Partition |
| Truman Capote ★ | In Cold Blood, Breakfast at Tiffany's | Nonfiction told like a novel, the scene before the crime |
| Raymond Carver ★ | What We Talk About When We Talk About Love, Cathedral | Saying less, endings that stop rather than finish |
| E. B. White ★ | Here Is New York, Once More to the Lake, Charlotte's Web | Clean essays, a place at a moment in time |

|---|---|---|
| Toni Morrison ★ | Beloved, Sula, The Bluest Eye, Song of Solomon | Places as characters, openings that refuse to explain |
| Kazuo Ishiguro ★ | The Remains of the Day, Never Let Me Go | Narrators who can't admit what they feel |
| Cormac McCarthy ★ | The Road, Blood Meridian, No Country for Old Men | No punctuation, landscape as threat |

| Chuck Palahniuk ★ | Fight Club, Choke, Consider This | Openings in the middle, repeated lines, minimalism |
| George Saunders ★ | Tenth of December, Lincoln in the Bardo, A Swim in a Pond in the Rain | Voice, kindness, how to revise |
| Zadie Smith ★ | White Teeth, On Beauty, Changing My Mind | Big families, essays with a sense of humour |

| Vivek Shanbhag ★ | Ghachar Ghochar, Sakina's Kiss (trans. Srinath Perur) | Money and family, small books with sharp turns |
| Jhumpa Lahiri ★ | Interpreter of Maladies, The Namesake | Quiet migrations, food as memory |
| Salman Rushdie ★ | Midnight's Children, Knife | Energy, narrators who can't stop talking |

| Elena Ferrante ★ | My Brilliant Friend | Friendship and rivalry, anger on the page |
| Milan Kundera | The Unbearable Lightness of Being | Ideas in the middle of a love story |
| Hilary Mantel ★ | Wolf Hall | Close third person, history in the present tense |
| Donna Tartt ★ | The Secret History, The Goldfinch | Prologues that confess the crime |
| Ocean Vuong ★ | On Earth We're Briefly Gorgeous | The novel as a letter, tenderness |
| Sally Rooney | Normal People, Conversations with Friends | Dialogue without quotation marks, the unsaid |
| Ottessa Moshfegh ★ | My Year of Rest and Relaxation, Eileen | Unlikeable narrators you can't put down |
| Jenny Offill | Dept. of Speculation | Fragments, white space, a marriage in short bursts |
| Lydia Davis ★ | Collected Stories | One-line stories, precision |
| Rachel Cusk | Outline | Characters revealed by what other people tell them |
| Annie Ernaux ★ | The Years, A Man's Place | Memory without sentiment, the plain style |
